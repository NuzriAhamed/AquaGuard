Research overview

Problem. Sri Lanka possesses 103 distinct river basins; the Kelani is the most heavily polluted, with over 7,000 industrial facilities discharging into its river valley. Simultaneously, inland recreational waterways, ancient tanks (wewas), and reservoirs expose bathers and tourists to catastrophic drowning risks, territorial wildlife confrontations (elephants, crocodiles), and dense crowding without automated alert infrastructure. Existing computer vision literature halts at single-frame detection bounding boxes, providing local authorities and lifeguards with zero actionable decision frameworks.

Aim. Architect an integrated multi-modal platform that detects environmental and physical hazards across inland waters and translates visual telemetry directly into decision metrics: density-weighted pollution scores, metric perimeter warnings, temporal drowning retention alarms, and grounded clinical first-aid triage.

Objective 1: Floating Waste & GDI

Quantify riverine debris with a danger-weighted Garbage Density Index (0-100) and track spatial distribution via GPS heatmaps.

Objective 2: Wildlife Metric Proximity

Protect bathers from wildlife attacks using an explicit 6-stage spatial pipeline computing boundary clearance in real-world meters.

Objective 3: Drowning Temporal Retention

Detect drowning incidents in video streams using a 10-call sliding window history that decouples alert state from database persistence.

Objective 4: Crowd Density & TankBot

Monitor crowd surges at historic tanks using ByteTrack person counts paired with a domain-restricted reservoir guidance assistant.

Objective 5: Emergency First-Aid CAG

Deliver domain-governed, WHO/Red Cross-grounded emergency first-aid advice across 5 trauma scenarios with vital-state cache invalidation.

Literature review and research gap

Five recent benchmark studies on floating-object detection were critically analyzed. While recent architectures iteratively improve mean average precision, none bridge the divide between raw bounding-box outputs and operational environmental governance. Furthermore, prior Sri Lankan water monitoring efforts (Wijayaweera et al., 2024) relied exclusively on chemical hardware sensors, lacking non-invasive computer vision capabilities.

Wildlife Proximity Engine & 6-Stage Spatial Pipeline

In response to formal supervisor review on spatial precision, the proximity engine was comprehensively overhauled from uncalibrated pixel heuristics into an academically rigorous 6-stage mathematical spatial pipeline. This resolves the 0-pixel centroid fallacy, introduces anti-ghosting overlap suppression, computes edge-to-edge physical boundary clearance, and dynamically calibrates measurements to real-world meters (m) via monocular perspective depth scaling.

Stage 1: Explicit 2D Matrix

Tracks actual 2D coordinates in global sensor space without arbitrarily fixing any entity as zero: B_animal = [x1a, y1a, x2a, y2a] and B_human = [x1h, y1h, x2h, y2h]. True centroids and ground foot-points are preserved continuously.

Stage 2: Anti-Ghosting IoU

Eliminates duplicate false detections when an animal entering the boundary triggers the human detector: if IoU > 0.55 AND distance(C_h, C_a) < 45 px, the duplicate bounding box artifact is purged immediately.

Stage 3: Boundary Clearance (d_edge)

Computes non-overlapping physical perimeter clearance: d_edge_px = √(Δx² + Δy²). When d_edge = 0.0m, physical contact or collision has occurred even if centroids remain 250px apart!

Stage 4: Perspective Depth Scaling

Calibrates real-world metric units using pinhole geometry anchored by standard adult standing height (H_human = 1.70 m): S_depth = 1.70 / (y2h - y1h) (m/px).

Automated Drowning Detection & Temporal Emergency Engine

1. Stream Inference

OpenCV extracts MJPEG stream (35ms frame interval) and processes through fine-tuned YOLOv8 with confidence threshold 0.40, separating drowning posture from active swimming.

2. 10-Call Temporal Logic

History window H_t aggregates 10 consecutive frame classifications: E_t = (d_t = 1) OR (sum(H_t) ≥ 2). The first hit triggers CRITICAL; 2 hits maintain CRITICAL across 8 subsequent empty frames.

3. Write Cooldown & Evidence

AlertManager implements an 8-second cooldown on SQLite writes, preventing database saturation during ongoing rescue operations while transmitting live Web Audio alarms.

Emergency First-Aid Chatbot Architecture

1. Five Trauma Scenarios

Domain-governed protocols for drowning submersion, severe hemorrhage, bone fractures, crocodile/snake bites, and heatstroke in remote reservoir locations.

2. Typed Vital Tracking

Finite-state machine records consciousness, breathing, and bleeding. Unobserved vitals remain explicitly unknown rather than inferred false.

3. WHO/Red Cross Grounding

RAG pipeline queries strictly verified medical corpus. Employs conservative abstention to decline answering rather than hallucinating procedures.

4. CAG State Invalidation

Cache key binds vital state. Any transition in victim vitals (e.g. breathing stops) instantly purges cached answers, forcing live emergency re-triage.

Garbage Density Index (GDI) explained

The Garbage Density Index calculates ecological impact via danger-weighted classification: GDI = Σ(Count × Confidence × Weight) ÷ Area Normalization × 20, bounded between 0 and 100. Weights reflect aquatic environmental degradation: Polythene (1.0), Plastic (0.8), Glass (0.7), and Metal (0.6). Severity scales: Low (0-25), Moderate (25-50), High (50-75), Critical (75-100).

Anticipated viva examination questions

Authoritative technical responses to anticipated panel questions regarding system novelty, computer vision trade-offs, spatial precision, and clinical safety boundaries:

All five benchmarked papers halt at bounding-box detection accuracy without decision frameworks. AquaGuard introduces the danger-weighted Garbage Density Index (GDI), geolocation-based temporal tracking, and integrates three additional public-safety neural modules into a single unified platform.

Centroid distance models objects as dimensionless geometric points. Large wildlife (such as elephants or bears occupying 350 to 450 pixels in sensor width) make physical contact at their outer perimeter where physical clearance gap d_edge reaches 0.0 meters, while their geometric center is still over 200 pixels away! Measuring physical boundary clearance (d_edge_px = √(Δx² + Δy²)) eliminates life-threatening alert latency.

In benchmark Frame 125, an elephant was only 16 px (approx. 8 cm) from a human bather, but centroid distance was 290.8 px. Because 290.8 px exceeded the legacy 220 px threshold, the legacy system erroneously remained in WARNING. The overhauled 6-stage engine evaluates dual criteria: since boundary clearance d_edge = 0.08m ≤ 1.5m and metric distance d_meters = 1.51m ≤ 3.5m, the engine escalates immediately and correctly to CRITICAL, triggering the emergency siren.

The system applies the temporal rule E_t = (d_t = 1) OR (sum(H_t) ≥ 2) across a 10-call sliding history window. A single positive frame immediately triggers CRITICAL to safeguard human life, while two positive detections maintain the CRITICAL alert state across up to 8 subsequent empty frames, preventing alert flicker during splashes or temporary submersion. An 8-second write cooldown prevents database saturation.

The CAG cache key binds (guidance_version, scenario_class, language, patient_state). If the bystander reports any change in patient condition (e.g. victim loses consciousness or stops breathing), the previous cache entry is immediately invalidated, preventing dangerous reuse of stale instructions and instantly triggering CPR escalation protocols.

Using perspective pinhole geometry anchored by standard human anatomical dimensions (adult standing height H_human = 1.70 m). By computing detected human bounding box pixel height h_human = y2 - y1, the engine dynamically calculates the depth scale factor S_depth = 1.70 / h_human (m/px), naturally converting both centroid and boundary clearance into physical meters.

Decoupling into Animaldetect.pt and Human.pt permits independent optimization of anchor priors, confidence thresholds, and inference parameters for quadruped wildlife versus upright human bathers without mutual feature interference.

Under the strict medical boundary, traditional remedies are never permitted to delay emergency ambulance dispatch (1990 Suwa Seriya). Cultural remedies are restricted to secondary, harmless supplementary practices only after standard medical care is administered, and are excluded entirely in acute crises like venomous snakebites or drowning.
